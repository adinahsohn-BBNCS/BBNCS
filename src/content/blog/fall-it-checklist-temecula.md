Summer schedules, vacations, and temporary workarounds have a way of sticking around. By fall, many Temecula businesses are back at full speed — and small IT gaps that felt harmless in August can turn into outages or security problems in October.

Use this checklist to get ahead before year-end crunch time.

## 1. Confirm backups actually restore

A backup that has not been tested is a guess.

- Check that nightly or hourly jobs completed successfully this month
- Restore one file and one folder — not just glance at a green dashboard icon
- Confirm off-site or cloud copies are separate from your main network

If restore takes longer than your team can afford to wait, talk with your IT partner about recovery time goals. Our [managed backup](/managed-backup-solutions/) clients get monitored jobs plus recovery planning — not just storage.

## 2. Patch what everyone forgot

Laptops that traveled, remote machines, and office servers all drift out of date.

- Windows/macOS updates applied
- Browser and common apps (Adobe, Zoom, Office) current
- Firmware on firewalls and Wi‑Fi gear reviewed

[Managed IT](/managed-it-services/) handles this continuously; if you are still break-fix, at least schedule a fall patch window before holiday traffic picks up.

## 3. Reset access and passwords

Fall is a good time to clean up who still has access to what.

- Remove former employees and contractors from email, VPN, and cloud apps
- Enable **multi-factor authentication** on email and admin accounts
- Rotate shared passwords (Wi‑Fi, vendor portals, generic inboxes)

Most breaches start with stolen credentials — MFA is one of the highest-return fixes for small businesses.

## 4. Review email and phishing risk

Phishing spikes around holidays and busy seasons.

- Remind staff: verify unexpected invoices, gift-card requests, and “urgent CEO” emails
- Check that spam filtering is enabled and not silently failing
- Make sure everyone knows who to call if they clicked something suspicious

If you do not have a written “what to do if…” note, add one page to your internal docs this month.

## 5. Plan for hardware that will not survive Q4

Aging PCs and servers fail at the worst times.

- List machines over 4–5 years old still in daily use
- Note any device that freezes, overheats, or cannot run current OS versions
- Budget replacements before tax season and holiday deadlines stack up

Replacing one problem machine before it dies beats emergency shopping during a outage.

## 6. Know who you will call after hours

Emergencies do not wait for Monday.

- Confirm your [IT support](/it-support-services/) contact for nights and weekends
- Post the number where staff can find it (not only on one manager’s phone)
- Test that new hires know how to open a ticket or reach help
